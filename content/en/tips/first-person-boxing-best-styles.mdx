## S-Tier: The Meta Definers

The top of the ladder belongs to the speed-control styles. These are the picks that dictate range in every exchange: fast enough to jab-and-slip before heavier styles can wind up, with specials that either extend combos or reset stamina pressure. In bracket play they win the bully matchup by simply never being where the power shots land. If your spin lands one of these, main it immediately — every hour invested here transfers directly to ranked wins. Their one weakness is impatience: they reward players who learned the [fundamentals drill](/guide/first-person-boxing-roblox-guide) and punish button-mashing with stamina crashes in round three.

## A-Tier: The Counter Styles

One step below sit the counterpuncher styles: slightly slower templates built around punishing aggression. Their specials trigger off successful slips and blocks, which makes them brutal into the meta's bullies but awkward against patient players who refuse to swing first. A-tier is where skill ceiling matters most — the same style can look tier-one in a counterplayer's hands and tier-three in a brawler's. If you enjoy the defensive side of the genre's [training fundamentals](/tips/boxing-tips), these rolls are keepers.

## B-Tier: The Bruisers

The heavy styles. Enormous damage per clean connection, short reach, slow windups. Into any style that respects range they struggle; into chaos they thrive. Bruisers are the best teaching styles in the game — playing one forces you to learn feints, corner work and stamina abuse because you cannot out-speed anything. They are also the most fun to clip. Keep one trained as a pocket pick for matchups where the opponent has never learned to move backward.

## Building Your Combo Game

Combos in First Person Boxing are frame arithmetic, not muscle memory: every style has a fastest opener, a window where the opponent's block is broken, and a damage cap before your stamina crashes the exchange. The universal three-step build: pick your fastest safe opener (usually the jab string), identify the special-cancel window in your style's moveset, and end on the heaviest punch your stamina bar survives. Test the timing in the lobby against AI before spending ranked points on it.

Style-specific combo notes matter at the top: S-tier speed styles cancel specials into themselves, effectively doubling safe damage; A-tier counters convert every successful slip into a full punish string; B-tier bruisers want corner carry — one clean heavy into a wall is an entire round of damage. ## Matchup Math: Why the Tiers Move

The tier order is not arbitrary — it follows three measurable properties. **Frames per jab** decides who controls neutral: the fastest safe opener in any exchange belongs to the style with the shorter windup, and S-tier styles win that race outright. **Special uptime** decides who controls rounds: specials that recharge mid-combat create pressure loops, while one-shot specials only convert. **Stamina tax per power shot** decides who owns round three: heavy styles pay double for the same commitment, which is why bruiser matchups flip hard late even when the early rounds look even.

When the developer tunes any of these three numbers — and every balance patch has touched at least one — the tier list shifts. ## Common Style Mistakes to Unlearn

Most First Person Boxing players lose fights before the bell, in the style screen. Four mistakes account for nearly all of them. **Chasing rarity over fit**: a rare style whose game plan clashes with your instincts performs worse than a common one you pilot naturally — the tier list describes ceilings, not guarantees. **Swapping mains every patch**: every relearn resets fifty fights of matchup knowledge; ride your main through one bad tuning note before jumping ship. **Upgrading evenly across a closet**: concentration beats coverage early — one maxed main outearns four half-built alts in every bracket. **Ignoring the stamina line on power shots**: bruiser mains especially need to internalize which heavies are affordable in round one versus round three, because the same punch is a win condition early and a suicide note late.

Unlearn those four and the tiers take care of themselves. The roll is random; the pilot is not — and in First Person Boxing, the pilot is what the ladder actually ranks.
